“Spectrum” describes the wide range and combination of ways autism can show up. It is not a single line from “a little autistic” to “very autistic,” and it does not mean that every autistic person has the same traits.

People can have different communication styles, sensory experiences, interests, and support needs. Those needs may also vary between areas of life and change with a person's environment, health, stress, or available support. Someone may be independent in one situation and need substantial help in another.

The spectrum is best understood as an individual profile, not a ranking. A diagnosis alone cannot tell us what a person can do or what support will help; we need to listen to that person and understand their specific circumstances.
