Autism is commonly associated with differences in two broad areas:

- Social communication and interaction, such as differences in conversational rhythm, interpreting nonverbal cues, or approaching social situations.
- Restricted or repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities, such as a strong preference for predictability, repeated movements or speech, or deeply focused interests.

Many autistic people also experience sensory information differently. A person may be more or less sensitive to sounds, light, touch, movement, or other sensations.

These are broad patterns, not a checklist that looks the same for everyone. Autistic people have varied personalities, abilities, and ways of relating to others; no single characteristic defines a person. Only a qualified professional can assess whether someone meets diagnostic criteria.
