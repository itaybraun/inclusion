It can be both, and autistic people do not all describe their experience in the same way. Autism is a different neurodevelopmental profile and, for many people, it is also a disability because it can involve substantial support needs or barriers in environments designed for non-autistic people.

Recognizing autism as a difference can affirm identity and challenge the idea that there is only one acceptable way to communicate or experience the world. Recognizing disability can make support needs and barriers visible and help protect access to accommodations and services. One perspective does not have to erase the other.

Language preferences vary. Some people prefer “autistic person,” while others prefer “person with autism.” When possible, use the words the person chooses, and avoid assuming what autism means for any individual.
