A meltdown is an overwhelmed response, not a deliberate request to test limits. Reduce demands, protect physical safety, and give the child a calm route away from the main pressure. Speak briefly, allow extra processing time, and return to learning only after the child is regulated.

## Immediate steps

- Check for danger to the child or others.
- Lower noise, audience, and verbal demands.
- Offer a known quiet place or break with clear options.
- Use the child's established calming plan.
- Document triggers and recovery afterward.

## What to avoid

- Punishing distress.
- Arguing, crowding, restraining, or demanding immediate compliance.
- Announcing the episode to peers.

## If the situation escalates

Follow the school's crisis plan if there is imminent danger, injury, or inability to maintain safety.
